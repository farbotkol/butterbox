# Usability findings

Severity is about completion of hospitality tasks. High findings repeatedly slow or obstruct common work. Medium findings create avoidable confusion, recovery effort or occasional rework. This review did not assess conformance across every state or establish a security incident.

| Rank | Severity | Finding and evidence | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | High | **The roster edit opens a different document state.** Grid slots, layout and draft editor already exist. Adding/editing is anchored by URL/query state and renders a separate form region above the grid. The control and result can be far apart on long rosters. Inspect the workspace links and editor at the form's position. [Roster workspace](../../src/app/roster/page.tsx) · [Shift editor](../../src/components/shift-editor.tsx) | Keep the grid, editor and status visible together. Start with one contextual editor, direct add affordance, predictable focus/scroll return and one Save draft. |
| 2 | High | **Rich roster features compete for attention.** Department selection, date, view, search, copy, publication history, approval and open shifts occupy separate sections. Planning takes a different route and directs editing back to the operational roster. The quick-change implementation already separates preview, apply, uncertain result and undo. [Operational roster](../../src/app/roster/page.tsx) · [Planning](../../src/app/roster/planning/page.tsx) · [Quick changes](../../src/components/roster-quick-changes.tsx) | One scoped toolbar, predictable Week/Day/Planning lenses, conflict summary and a visible selection bar. Keep the existing role restrictions and preview semantics. |
| 3 | High | **Time review crosses separate task surfaces.** Attendance exposes corrections and location exceptions, Work time exposes recorded time and corrections, and Timesheet review exposes department approval, payroll history and exports. A busy manager must infer where to resolve each item. [Attendance](../../src/app/attendance/page.tsx) · [Work time](../../src/app/work-time/page.tsx) · [Timesheets](../../src/app/timesheets/page.tsx) | Present one queue with actionable statuses and an original-versus-corrected review panel. | 4 | High | **Venue settings require separate decisions and saves.** Planning week, publication mode, conflict rules, location and clock-location policy have split forms and histories. Some actions are intentionally separate security or policy decisions. The page provides saved/error feedback through query state. [Venue settings](../../src/app/settings/organisation/page.tsx) | Group by venue and consequence. Give each independently editable section one clear save and a focused outcome. Consider combined submit only with an explicit partial-success and retry contract. |
| 5 | High | **Navigation foregrounds available functions more readily than today's tasks.** The source owner menu has 20 destinations including Help. Managers and employees see different subsets and open a menu at narrow widths. The role matrix and URL context helper are good foundations. [Navigation model](../../src/lib/navigation.ts) · [Menu](../../src/components/main-navigation.tsx) | Put Roster in the manager's first group and Clock, Schedule and Inbox at employee fingertips. Use a task-led Today screen with scoped continuation links. |
| 6 | Medium | **Profile context is split.** The directory and focused profile already replace a monolithic list. Employment has a separate editor route, while roles and work preferences use other patterns. Business permissions uses another member editor. [People](../../src/app/people/page.tsx) · [Profile](../../src/app/people/[employeeId]/page.tsx) · [Employment](../../src/app/people/[employeeId]/employment/page.tsx) · [Permissions](../../src/app/settings/permissions/page.tsx) | Make the person the stable context. [Roster](../../src/app/roster/page.tsx) · [Review panel](../../src/components/roster-review-panel.tsx) | Start from one publish review showing eligible draft changes, recipients, blockers and accurate outcomes. | 8 | Medium | **The mobile sign-in form starts below the branding panel.** Browser screenshots show the primary sign-in button below the 390×844 first viewport on the showcase. | 9 | Medium | **Shared error and loading treatment is inconsistent.** The route inventory shows loading/error boundaries for only a subset of high-traffic routes. Several actions use full-page query feedback, while the draft editor preserves invalid form values and shows field-adjacent errors. | 10 | Medium | **Payroll and reporting are organised around records rather than readiness.** Users can find period history, exports and provider mapping, but moving among them changes context. Keep source data and exports auditable, and leave external connection consent and simulation boundaries intact. |

## Strengths to preserve

- The roster already has cell-level entry, day/weekly rendering, keyboard help and quick-change preview. The design should make these easier to find and keep their server behaviour intact.
- The shell supports role-dependent labels, selected route semantics and reduced-motion handling. Venue and business context is encoded explicitly on navigation.
- The draft shift editor keeps typed values when validation fails. Time screens expose original events, approval decisions and export provenance.
- Reusable styling starts with semantic colour tokens, Tailwind and Lucide. Unify existing patterns before adding a parallel styling stack.

## How the problems show up by workflow

| Workflow | Current treatment | Design priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roster day and week | The week combines people, availability and shift targets. Day adds a time-scale view. Phone shows one day beside the pinned team column. Copy created eight drafts from a chosen week with one save in the fixture. | Make Day and Week equally easy to reach. Keep active filters visible and expose the destination and effect of bulk operations before applying. |
| Movement and conflict | Clicking Move or duplicate opens a proposal and a cancel path. Drag to a day also opened a proposal. Preview and undo both stayed on the roster. Source includes advisory and blocking conflict outcomes. | Keep Cancel and Undo near their operation. Label constraint type and show why a person is unavailable. A keyboard and touch action must reach the same authorised outcome. |
| Department, job role and permissions | Department links lead to roles and job-role detail. Business member access uses a separate scope editor. These surfaces distinguish assigning a job role from granting permissions. | Show assignment and access terminology consistently and keep permission changes deliberate. Retain separate audit history and explicitly label current scope. |
| Employee self-service | Schedule, availability, shift swaps, conversations and inbox have their own pages. The phone schedule labelling includes published-only visibility. The browser reached the schedule in two clicks from Dashboard and verified clock-in and clock-out in two additional attendance actions from the clock page. | Surface upcoming shift, clock status and pending requests in Today, with links that keep the target shift or review item in context. |
| Reporting and notifications | The scoped inbox contains source-specific actions and links. Hours, overtime exceptions and demand history expose different filters and evidence. Report availability depends on access and data. | Order operational decisions first and offer relevant drillthrough to the canonical record. Put advanced demand and export actions under a consistent reporting surface. Preserve unread and acknowledgement semantics. |
| Entry, empty and private states | Sign-in, password recovery, missing invitation and unpaired kiosk are explicit views. Scope-dependent screens can redirect or return a rendered availability message. | Use accessible headings and actionable messages consistently. Route a genuine empty state to the next permitted action, with no suggestion that a protected record exists. |
